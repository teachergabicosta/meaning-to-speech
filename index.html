01 Retrieve form

0–6 min · Recall before you look for help

Answer each prompt aloud using a complete sentence. Try not to write a script first.

Routine

What do you usually do at the beginning of your workday?

Recent past

What did you work on yesterday or last week?

Current situation

How are things going at work this week?

Challenge

What is one thing you don't usually do at work? Why not?

Useful language to retrieve

I usually…I don't usually…Yesterday, I…I was…I didn't…I had to…

Try first without looking at the chunks. Use them only if you get stuck.

Stage 1 of 6
02

02 Connect meaning

6–14 min · Decide what you want to communicate

Before building a sentence, identify the relationship between the ideas.

Reason

Explain why something happens.

because

The meeting was postponed because the client was unavailable.

Contrast

Show that two ideas are different or unexpected.

but

The deadline was tight, but we delivered on time.

Result

Explain what happened as a consequence.

so

The data was incomplete, so we checked the report again.

Choose the relationship

1. The supplier changed the delivery date. We informed the customer.

Use so: informing the customer is the result of the supplier changing the date.

2. I wanted to join the call. I had another meeting at the same time.

Use but to contrast your intention with the scheduling conflict.

3. We asked for more time. The figures needed to be checked.

Use because: needing to check the figures explains why you asked for more time.

Your turn

Choose one relationship and make it true for your own work.

03 Model an idea

14–22 min · Notice how the message develops

Compare the two versions. Read both aloud and notice how the second version makes the relationship clearer.

Disconnected

The report was late. The data was missing. I contacted the team. We sent it today.

Connected

The report was late because some data was missing. I contacted the team, so we were able to send it today.

Break the message into meaning blocks

Situation / problem

The report was late…

Reason

…because some data was missing.

Action

I contacted the team…

Result

…so we were able to send it today.

Notice the structure

Past situationbecause + reasonactionso + result
Recall the model without looking

Explain aloud: What was the problem? Why did it happen? What action did the speaker take? What was the result?

04 Guided transformation

22–36 min · Adapt the message to your own context

Do not copy the model word for word. Keep the communication pattern, but change the details and meaning.

Round A — Use the prompts

Situation: A meeting changed.

Chunks: The meeting was moved…because…so I…

Situation: You had a difficult task, but completed it.

Chunks: The task was…but…I managed to…

Situation: You couldn't finish something on time.

Chunks: I didn't…because…so I…

Round B — Less support

Choose one situation and speak for 20–30 seconds. Use at least two connected ideas.

Project update

What is happening, and what needs attention?

Team communication

What did you discuss or clarify recently?

Work priorities

What was important this week, and why?

Stage 6 of 6
01

Retrieve form

Recall useful language

Answer aloud: What do you usually do first in the morning? How often do you do something you enjoy? What do you prefer doing after work? What do you rarely do?

02

Connect meaning

Choose what you want to express

Routine: I usually… · Frequency: I sometimes / rarely… · Preference: I prefer… to… · Reason: …because it helps me…

Try it: Choose the clearest sentence: “I sometimes exercise during the week.” / “I exercise always.” / “I am sometimes exercise.”
03

Model an idea

Notice how the answer expands

Short: “I like walking.” Connected: “I usually go for a walk after work because it helps me clear my head. I sometimes listen to a podcast while I walk.”

Try it: Identify the routine, reason and extra detail.
04

Guided transformation

Build your own version

Complete aloud: I usually ___ because ___. I sometimes ___ when ___. I prefer ___ to ___ because ___.

Try it: Add one detail beginning with “For example…” or “Especially when…”
05

Speaking task

Talk about your real routine

Speak for 45–60 seconds. Include one routine, how often you do it, one preference with a reason, and a specific example.

Try it: Follow-ups: Why is that important to you? Has it always been your routine? What would you like to change?
06

Feedback + retry

Improve one connection

Check: Did you use a frequency adverb naturally? Did you explain a preference with because? Did you add a detail?

Try it: Repeat the answer using only three keywords. Record three useful chunks.
01

Retrieve form

Bring past forms back

Answer aloud: What did you do last weekend? What happened recently? Where did you go on your last day off?

Try it: Retrieve three past verbs: one regular, one irregular and one movement verb.
02

Connect meaning

Put events in a useful order

First = starting point · Then = next event · After that = another event · Finally = ending/result.

Try it: Order the events: We found a small restaurant. / We decided to explore the neighborhood. / We went home tired but happy. Then tell the story.
03

Model an idea

Notice the story shape

“Last Saturday, I met a friend downtown. First, we walked around the market. Then, we found a café and had lunch. After that, we visited a bookshop. Finally, we took the bus home because it started raining.”

Try it: Identify the time, first event, next event and ending.
04

Guided transformation

Build a story from keywords

Map: When/where ___ · First ___ · Then ___ · After that ___ · Finally ___. Choose a real or invented event.

Try it: Retell it, but change the ending or add an unexpected event.
05

Speaking task

Tell a short story

Speak for 60–90 seconds about a day that did not go as planned, something you learned, an outing, or a problem you solved.

Try it: Follow-ups: What happened next? How did you feel? Would you do anything differently?
06

Feedback + retry

Make the sequence clearer

Check: past forms for completed events; at least three sequence markers; clear ending.

Try it: Retell without reading the full version. Note one sentence to improve.
01

Retrieve form

Recall past events

Answer: What were you doing yesterday at 8 p.m.? Tell me about a time something unexpected happened. What happened the last time you were in a hurry?

02

Connect meaning

Scene or main event?

Background: “I was walking home…” Main event: “…when it started to rain.” Background describes an action in progress; the main event tells what happened.

Try it: Which part is the background and which is the main event in: “I was getting off the bus when I dropped my phone”?
03

Model an idea

Add a scene to the event

Only event: “I dropped my phone.” Context + event: “I was getting off the bus when I dropped my phone, so I had to go back and look for it.”

Try it: Notice background, event and result. Use past continuous only when it helps.
04

Guided transformation

Add missing context

Build: I was ___ when ___ happened. While I was ___, ___. I couldn’t ___ because ___.

Try it: Add where you were, who was there, or how you reacted.
05

Speaking task

Explain what was going on

Speak for about a minute about something that went wrong, unexpected news, or an ordinary moment with a surprise. Set the scene, report the event and explain what happened next.

Try it: Follow-ups: What were you doing before that? What happened immediately after? How did you respond?
06

Feedback + retry

Keep the timeline clear

Check whether the listener can picture the moment and identify the main event.

Try it: Retell and note one improved sentence or useful chunk.
01

Retrieve form

Recall comparison language

Compare two ways of relaxing. Which is more useful: planning ahead or being flexible? What is easier now than five years ago?

02

Connect meaning

Identify what matters

Compare online course (flexible, lower cost, independent) with in-person course (fixed schedule, higher cost, direct interaction). Criteria: time, cost, comfort, quality, flexibility.

Try it: Choose two criteria that matter to you and explain why.
03

Model an idea

Compare, then justify

“Both options can be useful, but the online course is more flexible and usually less expensive. The in-person course offers more direct interaction, whereas online study gives me more control over my schedule. I would choose the online course because flexibility matters most to me.”

Try it: Notice both, comparative forms, whereas and the decision.
04

Guided transformation

Build a fair comparison

Complete: ___ is more ___ than ___. Both ___, but ___. ___ is less ___, whereas ___.

Try it: Finish: I would choose ___ because ___. Another reason is ___.
05

Speaking task

Recommend one option

Compare two apps, two ways to spend a free afternoon, two ways to learn a skill, or two travel options. Explain at least two criteria.

Try it: Follow-up: What would make you change your mind?
06

Feedback + retry

Make your reasoning convincing

Check: clear criteria, accurate comparative/contrast marker, at least two reasons.

Try it: Give your recommendation again, more concisely, without reading.
01

Retrieve form

Start with a clear position

Is it better to plan your week or decide day by day? What makes a meeting useful? Do people learn more from success or mistakes?

Try it: Answer briefly, then add one reason.
02

Connect meaning

Build an opinion chain

Position: I think… · Reason: because… · Example: For example… · Nuance: However…

Try it: Choose a topic and decide which two moves you can support with a real example.
03

Model an idea

Notice how evidence supports an opinion

“I think flexible working can be effective because people can organize their time around demanding tasks. For example, I focus more easily when I have fewer interruptions. However, teams still need time to communicate and make decisions together.”

Try it: Identify the opinion, reason, example and counterpoint.
04

Guided transformation

Support your own view

Choose: “Technology saves people time.” / “Routine is important.” / “Working in a team is better than working alone.” Build: I think ___ because ___. For example, ___. However, ___.

Try it: Now speak again using only three keywords.
05

Speaking task

Defend a view without memorizing

Speak for 60–90 seconds. Give your view, a reason, an example, and a qualification if it fits.

Try it: Follow-ups: Can you give me a specific example? Is that always true? What might someone who disagrees say?
06

Feedback + retry

Make the reasoning easier to follow

Check: clear position; example supports the reason; however marks a real contrast.

Try it: Repeat with a clearer connection between your opinion and evidence.
01

Retrieve form

Recall future language

What are you going to do this weekend? What do you think will change next year? What might stop you from doing something you want to do?

02

Connect meaning

Choose the future meaning

Plan: I’m going to… · Possibility: I might / could… · Prediction: I think it will… · Condition: If I have time, I’ll…

Try it: Choose a real plan and decide which meaning types fit.
03

Model an idea

Connect a plan to a condition

“I’m going to take a short course next month. I might study in the evenings, but it depends on my schedule. If work gets busy, I’ll move some study time to the weekend.”

Try it: Identify plan, possibility, condition and result.
04

Guided transformation

Make your plan realistic

In the next few weeks, I’m going to ___ because ___. I might ___ if ___. If ___ happens, I’ll ___ instead.

Try it: Say the plan once, then change the condition and adapt the result.
05

Speaking task

Explain an adaptable plan

Speak for 60 seconds about a goal, weekend plan or small change. Include an intention, one possibility and a condition.

Try it: Follow-ups: Why? What could make it difficult? What will you do if the plan changes?
06

Feedback + retry

Match form to meaning

Check: going to for plan/intention; might/could for possibility; present form after if in a real future condition.

Try it: Repeat and note one useful future chunk.
01

Retrieve form

Retrieve problem-solving language

What would you do if your internet stopped during an important call? If you had two deadlines on the same day? If you could not find a booking confirmation?

Try it: Try: I need to… / I could… / I would ask…
02

Connect meaning

Explain before solving

Situation: The issue is… · Impact: That means… · Request: Could you…? · Option: We could…

Try it: Choose one scenario and explain the situation in one sentence before suggesting a solution.
03

Model an idea

Notice a practical exchange

A: “I’m having trouble opening the shared file. It says I don’t have access.” B: “Could you send me a screenshot? I can check the permissions.” A: “If that doesn’t work, we could send a new link.”

Try it: Notice the problem, request and alternative.
04

Guided transformation

Build a practical solution

I’m having trouble with ___. That means ___. Could you ___? We could ___. If that doesn’t work, we could ___.

Try it: Make the request polite and the next step specific.
05

Speaking task

Work through a problem together

Choose a delayed delivery, schedule conflict, broken app/device, or a task needing more time. Explain, ask a clarifying question and suggest two next steps.

Try it: Goal: reach a practical next step, not just list advice.
06

Feedback + retry

Make the next step specific

Check: clear problem, polite request or clarification, realistic next step.

Try it: Repeat the exchange using your best request and solution chunk.
01

Retrieve form

Retrieve conversation keepers

Complete aloud: Let me think… / What I mean is… / I’m not sure how to say it, but… / Could you explain what you mean?

Try it: Use two chunks in short spoken responses.
02

Connect meaning

Choose a repair strategy

A: You cannot remember one word. B: You do not understand the other person. C: Your answer is confusing and needs restating.

Try it: Match each situation with paraphrase, clarification or restatement.
03

Model an idea

Notice repair in a real exchange

A: “What do you like about that place?” B: “I like the… I’m not sure of the word. It’s a place where people borrow books.” A: “A library?” B: “Yes, exactly. I like the quiet atmosphere there.”

Try it: Notice that the speaker keeps communicating without the exact word.
04

Guided transformation

Explain without the exact word

Describe something without naming it: It’s something you use to… / It’s a place where people… / It’s a kind of activity that…

Try it: Pause deliberately and continue with “What I mean is…” or “Let me put it another way…”
05

Speaking task

Have a 4–5 minute conversation

Choose a skill you learned, a place you recommend, a recent decision or a routine you want to change. Answer, give reasons, ask follow-ups and paraphrase when needed.

Try it: Use “What about you?”, ask for an example, or clarify a point.
06

Feedback + retry

Notice what helped you continue

Check: thinking-time chunk; paraphrase; follow-up or clarification question.

Try it: Identify one moment when you repaired or extended an answer.
01

Retrieve form

Retrieve your strongest chunks

Without opening Discover first, say one chunk for a reason, an example, a contrast and repair. Then check the reference.

Try it: Record the chunks you recalled.
02

Connect meaning

Choose a message worth sharing

Choose one route: experience (what happened/learned), opinion (what you think/why), plan (what you intend/what may change), or decision (compare options/justify).

Try it: Write three to six keywords, not full sentences.
03

Model an idea

Build a connected message

Map: Core idea (I think / Recently, I / I’m going to…) → Connection (because / first then / however) → Support (for example / one reason is) → Close (so / that’s why / what I learned was).

Try it: Model: “Recently, I decided to change my routine because I wanted more time to focus. For example, I now plan the most important tasks first. It is not always easy, but I’ve noticed that I feel more organized.”
04

Guided transformation

Prepare with minimal support

Message map: main idea; reason/context; example/sequence/comparison; closing thought.

Try it: Put your notes aside and prepare to answer an unexpected follow-up: Why? What happened next? What would you change? What if…?
05

Speaking task

Final talk + follow-up

Give a 2–3 minute talk and answer two follow-up questions. Connect at least two meaning moves and use repair strategies if needed.

Try it: Do not restart from the beginning after every small mistake.
06

Feedback + retry

Compare your progress with Lesson 1

Check: connect idea to reason/example; organize a story/explanation; answer follow-ups; keep going when a word is missing.

Try it: What feels easier now? What is still difficult? Choose one target for the next month.
